# Personal design defaults

These are this user's cross-project preferences, explicitly requested to persist. Apply them before proposing or delegating frontend design. A newer request or an established project's approved identity takes precedence; do not require the user to restate these defaults, and do not turn them into a single house theme.

## Visual character

- For early drafts of a new visual direction, favor bold, unusual, idea-rich concepts over a conservative default. Give the product's meaning a distinctive expression through composition, imagery and interaction; refinement can reduce intensity after the idea is visible. This is permission to explore, not a requirement for maximalism, extra draft rounds or animation everywhere. Preserve purpose, useful content, usability, accessibility, intentional cropping and the checks appropriate to the draft's fidelity. Routine repairs and refinement of an approved design keep their established boundaries.
- Avoid cream/beige/tinted-paper as the automatic background. Liking elegant typography or newspaper character does not imply liking aged paper. Choose the palette deliberately with the user; neither dark mode nor neon is the automatic replacement.
- Favor character that feels authored: meaningful symbols, small discoveries, playful HTML-era interactions, culturally specific voice and details with a setup/payoff. A modest effect can be memorable. Do not manufacture personality through generic quirky slogans or forced jokes.
- Favor useful visuals integrated with the idea over scattered captions that repeat it. Apply `SKILL.md`'s purpose and repetition boundaries across new projects; they preserve meaningful small text, symbols and expression rather than impose a plain house style.
- Reason from the physical world when it helps a design feel coherent: how light meets a surface, what a material suggests, how movement starts and settles, and what an icon represents. Explain the expected relationship, try it in context, and revise from the rendered result. Treat perceptual explanations as hypotheses rather than universal facts; readability and usability take precedence over literal simulation. Treat typography, color fields, backgrounds, accents, symbols, composition and transitions as a coherent whole. Expressive typography and useful diagrams are welcome, but no specific font or illustration style is a universal requirement.
- For an expressive front-page illustration, favor behavior that completes its idea rather than stopping at its appearance. Infer the response from the relationship, material or metaphor you chose: what could the visitor discover by engaging with it, or what could one brief event reveal? Choose the trigger and behavior together with the composition. A generic entrance, button hover or unrelated panel transition does not complete that illustration. Stillness is a deliberate alternative when it conveys the idea better or protects the task; reduced-motion and coarse-pointer versions must remain understandable and usable. This preference does not prescribe an effect, require literal simulation or animate every illustration.
- Give content breathing room through considered gutters, grouping and rhythm. Large empty regions do not compensate for text crowded against an edge. Dividers, boxes, labels and split layouts need an explained role rather than functioning as automatic editorial decoration.
- Keep exploration open: expressive homepages, practical reference pages and experimental portfolios may have different needs. Related pages can differ in treatment while retaining recognizable connections. Do not force every project into restrained documentation styling or every reading page into a spectacle.

## Interaction and collaboration

- Mouse activation must not add or leave an incidental focus highlight on any interactive element, including buttons, links, selectors and text-entry fields. Changing its color does not satisfy this preference. Verify actual pointer use across each control family, then keyboard, programmatic restoration and mixed input separately; `:focus-visible` is a browser heuristic, including on text inputs. Preserve visible keyboard/programmatic focus, the caret, text selection, editing, validation, meaningful pressed/selected states and user-controlled high-contrast colors. Make those distinct states and hover feedback fit the visual system. [selector-feedback.md](selector-feedback.md) owns selector implementation and authored/native indicator boundaries; this preference applies beyond selectors. Intentional blue palettes remain valid.
- For readers and similar sustained-reading surfaces, favor continuous, finished feedback when navigating content or changing the index, disclosures and theme. Plan the whole feature's entry, exit and interruption before choosing parts; animating only its indicator can leave the experience feeling abrupt or cheap. Preserve orientation, immediate input response and reduced-motion behavior. Apply this preference before implementation, while leaving the treatment open: it does not require every element to animate or one particular scrolling, icon or reveal effect.
- When a control changes its state symbol and motion is allowed, use a continuous morph or transformation rather than an instant glyph swap. Keep its visual center stable and make reversal coherent. Choose suitable shared geometry or a premade morphing control; for a plus/minus, the horizontal stroke can stay while the vertical stroke shortens into its center. Respect reduced motion and retain clear state semantics; this does not authorize decorative motion on unrelated elements.
- When the design includes an authored text or textbox emphasis highlight, give the mark a fitting smooth animation, such as a sweep or draw, rather than stopping at a static colored background. Choose its trigger, timing and reset to suit the sentence and context; keep the words readable, wrapping stable and reduced motion usable. This is finishing for an already chosen highlight, not a requirement to add highlights, animate every heading or animate native text selection and validation cues. Animated underlines remain a liked option when a drawn mark fits the idea.
- Make unresolved visual decisions easy to see and try: prefer small visual comparisons over prose-only descriptions, with interactive controls when useful. Use side-by-side samples, before/after views, toggles, sliders or another fitting format; neither sliders nor a fixed number of options are mandatory. Follow [taste-selection.md](taste-selection.md) for comparable content, reset and keyboard behavior. This is a collaboration preference, not a universal aesthetic or a reason to reopen approved choices.
- Put useful actions where their context is understood. For teaching, associate practice with the actual concept/example and carry its state into the exercise. Name search/explanation actions by their benefit rather than defaulting to vague `?` or `Help` chrome. These preferences do not establish that all help buttons or separate workspaces are unusable.
- Explain consequential aesthetic and interaction choices, their purpose and tradeoff before substantial implementation; seek review rather than silently bundling unapproved choices with an approved reference. Purpose includes curiosity and delight, not only task efficiency. Routine repairs inside an approved system need no new approval round. Explicit delegation can authorize provisional choices.
- Prefer suitable premade components, starting with the project's installed parts, to rebuilding UI from scratch. Use [component-selection.md](component-selection.md) for targeted thematic discovery; a suitable part can stand alone. Show proposed new UI parts in an isolated preview before product integration by default, including delegated aesthetic choices; an explicit current preview waiver can override this. Routine repairs within an approved system need no new parts-selection round. [liked-motion-options.md](liked-motion-options.md) preserves reusable choices, including highlight sweeps, animated underlines and functional reader materials. Introducing these treatments remains optional; a chosen emphasis highlight follows the animation preference above. Do not apply the catalog everywhere.
- Do the reference filtering. Use a small relevant selection and explain transferable qualities; do not assign broad browsing or bookmark-sorting homework. Learn from designers' stated reasoning, not just screenshots, and distinguish source principles from evidence of this user's taste.
- For useful generated assets, the user's authenticated Codex native image-generation route is authorized; use it when an authored asset materially helps the design. Prefer the latest supported GPT Image model available through that route. Explicitly select the model when the tool exposes a selector; otherwise verify the route/default when available and leave the version unverified if it cannot be established. An unverified backend version alone does not revoke this native-route permission. Model selection belongs in tool configuration, not the artwork prompt; never claim a version from prompt text or an assumed default. This permission does not authorize credential extraction, an API-key/paid fallback, or publication. Generated textures and other taste-bearing assets still need rendered review before replacing approved assets.

## Scope boundaries

Keep project-specific content, assets, exact colors, jokes, selected fonts and runtime budgets in that project's context. A project's rejected feature or implementation does not become a global prohibition. An observed defect establishes a correction for the affected behavior; it does not establish an aesthetic preference, a required feature or a conclusion about model weights.
