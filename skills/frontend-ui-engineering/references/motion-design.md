# Motion Design and Review

Read while planning an interactive or expressive demonstration's states, not only after opting into animation. Also use when implementing, reviewing, or auditing UI motion. Preserve the project's motion tokens and accessible component primitives. No additional library is required for a simple transition.

When choosing expressive effects or material treatments, consult [liked-motion-options.md](liked-motion-options.md) for this user's reusable choices and their constraints. Use only the options that serve the current product; they do not require an animation pack.

## Decide before implementing

1. Form a motion design hypothesis before choosing effects. What idea should the visitor perceive, what physical or spatial behavior makes it believable, what should remain stable, and how should the response change while input persists, moves or ends? Put a short prediction in the design brief, choose a representation capable of producing it, then compare normal-speed playback with that prediction. Revise the representation or behavior when the result conveys the wrong idea, even if it is smooth and technically correct. For example, apparent volume depends on changing projected relationships between spatial parts; tilting coplanar shapes together can still read as a flat sheet. A material responding to sustained input can hold a deformation and recover on release; a passing event can resolve as a finite wave. These illustrate reasoning, not assigned effects or universal pointer mappings. Use liked options as possibilities to reason from, not a recipe to copy. Translate the request into every affected target, trigger, direction and state; animating one target or substituting another effect does not fulfill a multi-target request. Choose movement from the current product and illustration's meaning rather than applying the same entrance or hover response everywhere. Stillness is appropriate when motion contributes nothing.
2. Consider repetition and input method. Respond to navigation and commands immediately; this does not require the changed content to appear abruptly. Evaluate continuity at the change separately from stillness after settlement: a quiet reading surface can still carry navigation, opening/closing and theme changes continuously. When continuity helps follow an object, reading context or expressive demonstration, carry the content and associated indication through a brief coherent transition, applying the sustained-reading preference in `personal-design-defaults.md`. An animated button alone does not explain a replaced illustration or panel. Keep typing, precision operations and frequent commands efficient; never delay input, focus or availability until an animation finishes. Stillness remains appropriate when motion contributes nothing. Keyboard input alone is not a reason to remove useful state feedback.
3. Choose the smallest mechanism: CSS transitions for state changes, `@starting-style` for supported entry transitions, CSS keyframes for predetermined sequences, WAAPI for programmatic playback, or the existing motion library for gestures and coordinated springs. Check target-browser support and exit/unmount behavior; entry styling alone does not implement an exit lifecycle.
4. Define interruption, entry, exit, reduced-motion behavior, and verification before tuning the curve. For entry motion, decide when its target is stable and its meaningful frames can be seen: first paint, hydration, restored state, fonts or asset loading can change that point. Choose the smallest lifecycle or rendering mechanism that preserves the intended playback without delaying controls or hiding essential content. A fixed delay is not evidence of readiness; no universal hydration gate or wait for every asset is required. Treat entrance and exit as independent lifecycles with their own timing and state visibility; verify each requested direction rather than assuming a reversed entrance implements the requested exit.

## Tune without imposing a new style

- Reuse existing duration and easing tokens. As starting ranges, small feedback often needs roughly 100–200 ms and menus roughly 150–250 ms; larger surfaces may need longer. Judge actual distance, purpose, repetition, and device—not a universal duration ceiling.
- Prefer an immediate response with deceleration for entrances; smooth acceleration/deceleration can suit movement between established positions. Linear timing suits constant-rate progress. Custom curves and springs are options, not quality requirements.
- For trigger-anchored popovers or an object expanding into a detail view, relate origin and placement to the object that caused the change; keep unanchored dialogs centered. Group content that moves as one object and distinguish persistent, incoming and outgoing elements. Scale, opacity or a shared-element transition may help continuity; a pure fade or instant change is also valid. Do not mandate scale on every button or image.
- Specify transition properties instead of `transition: all`. Prefer transform and opacity where they preserve the required layout and text clarity. Do not substitute a scaled, distorted accordion for necessary layout change merely to avoid animating height.
- Rapid toggles, toast changes, and gestures must retarget from the current visual state rather than jump to an initial keyframe. Transitions or velocity-preserving springs often fit; deliberately controlled WAAPI/keyframe playback can also work. Test cancellation and reversal, not just the first successful playback.
- During animated navigation, distinguish a new navigation command from manual viewport input. Rapid next/previous or direct-target commands retarget travel from the current visual position while resolving each accepted command against the requested destination; stopping the old transport must not reset that destination or roll its indication back to an intermediate observed location. Genuine manual wheel, touch, scrollbar or navigation-key input instead adopts the user's observed position promptly. Cancel the mechanism moving the viewport, not only its tracking flag or settlement timer; delayed callbacks must not resume the destination or steal focus. Preserve input editing and keys consumed by other controls. Test real command bursts and reversals as well as manual input during travel, then verify indication and final position; a synthetic scroll event or a settled anchor test does not establish takeover. Native smooth scrolling is a candidate, not a cross-browser cancellation guarantee.
- Keep the cause and response perceptible at normal speed. A change present in CSS can be too slight to convey selection or continuity. Coordinate dependent movement so the visitor can follow the object: simultaneous translation, zoom, masking or replacement should have a coherent result, with sequential timing when it makes that relationship clearer. Maintain intentional crops and content visibility throughout; avoid exposed placeholders, unintentionally covering outgoing copy, detached connectors or multiple mechanisms fighting over one transform.
- Use stagger only when it clarifies grouping; never postpone interaction or apply a long cascade to routine results. Subsequent tooltips in a group can skip the initial delay, using the existing primitive's supported behavior.

## Accessibility and performance boundaries

- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by removing, reducing, or replacing nonessential motion. Instant state changes are valid; retain a short fade only when helpful and comfortable. Keep focus, announcements, and functionality equivalent. Include animated pseudo-elements such as `::backdrop` when relevant. When reduced-motion preference changes or printing begins, verify that in-flight CSS/WAAPI motion stops or reaches a usable static state; a zero-duration rule or empty animation list alone does not establish this.
- Gate decorative hover motion to hover-capable pointers without removing keyboard focus feedback. Test touch cancellation and pointer capture cleanup for drags; additional fingers must not jump the active gesture. Prefer the component library's gesture handling over a universal velocity threshold.
- Hardware acceleration depends on properties, browser, animation mechanism, and scene—not merely the presence of CSS or WAAPI. Profile under realistic main-thread load before rewriting Motion shorthand values or claiming a speedup. Layout and paint animations require measurement; blanket GPU guarantees are not evidence.
- Avoid inherited CSS-variable updates across large subtrees on every animation frame when a local style update suffices. Apply `will-change` selectively and remove temporary hints. Blur is not a default repair for a confusing state transition.
- If a trace implicates broad inherited color animation, investigate offscreen style work as well as visible paint. Containment is a candidate, not a drop-in fix: verify margin collapse, section/anchor geometry, resize/font changes, scrolling, focus, find-in-page and print. Keep only measured changes that preserve the required appearance and behavior; a diagnostic removal of approved motion is not a shipping fix.

## Verify and report

Exercise every requested target and state in normal playback, both directions where applicable, rapid reversal, repeated activation, exit/unmount, keyboard, touch where relevant, and reduced motion in a real browser with representative content. Verify visible intermediate behavior and content lifetime, not just final styles or source presence. Inspect slowed playback for jumps and origin errors, then judge responsiveness at normal speed. Deliver a playable recording or interactive preview under the skill's checks and visual review before commit. User approval of the look is not performance evidence.

For motion on entry or restored state, observe a fresh load and reload from the first visible frame through settlement, including relevant initialization and asset/font work. Check that the effect is seen once as intended rather than stalling, jumping ahead, flashing or restarting at hydration; essential content and input must remain available. A replay started after the page has settled does not test this lifecycle. Investigate intermittent stalls with comparable repeated runs and bounded active-window evidence; do not prescribe an arbitrary delay, remove approved expression or claim a loading speedup when only playback scheduling changed. When no entry motion exists, record that boundary rather than inventing an entrance or requiring a startup profile.

When layout motion overlaps navigation, verify the destination after both settle: a collapsing disclosure can move an already-measured anchor. Coordinate or settle that layout before measuring the destination, while preserving ordinary disclosure motion. Test intentionally instant neighboring disclosures and closed-content printing when changing shared selectors. Measure intermediate geometry, visibility and actual content lifetime; browser-owned pseudo-element motion may not appear in `getAnimations()`, so an empty animation list is not proof of no motion. Keep intermediate samples separate from settled-state comparisons.

For performance findings, profile the active transition window, not an idle-padded average or just the longer decorative tail. Compare repeated baselines and the candidate under the same content, viewport and environment; pair traces with an untraced timestamp-only rAF control so the sampler does not force layout reads. Callback cadence, compositor draws and presentation feedback are distinct signals, not proof of physically displayed FPS. Report the measured signal and window, browser/device scope and headless or physical-device limits.

Keep smoothness evidence separate from state correctness: sampled colors, a completed morph, rapid reversal and the final destination do not show frame continuity or manual takeover. Observe repeated normal-speed playback with representative content; investigate a visible stall or a material performance claim using active-window timing and, where needed, a trace. A component-store demo is a starting point, not the tested product: recheck after adapting theme, content, geometry or surrounding effects, and after repairs that can change their cost. Do not remove approved continuity merely to clear a timing check.

For a codebase audit, inventory actual motion with file/line evidence. Prioritize shared primitives and user-visible frequency, not raw occurrence counts. Each finding needs an observed defect or labeled risk, the smallest fix, and a concrete acceptance check. Record useful no-motion cases so the audit does not become a mandate to add animation everywhere. Review scope does not authorize implementation.
