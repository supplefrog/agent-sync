### Behavior-level selection

| Concern | Useful contribution | Synthesis and rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Visual direction | Anthropic: choices grounded in the subject, not stock category decoration | Short concrete thesis and one coherent direction. Reject forced novelty, negative-style blacklists, and “design lead” role-play as substitutes for decisions. |
| Different surface jobs | Impeccable entrypoint and `operate`: task completion, comprehension, persuasion, and exploration need different priorities | Surface-specific priorities in the owner. Product UI may be dense, familiar, and single-family; a campaign may be expressive. Reject landing-page art direction imposed on settings or docs. |
| Structure before skin | Hallmark `structure`: heading placement, content composition, dividers, action treatment, imagery, and motion are separate design choices | Content relationships choose table, workspace, document, gallery, demonstration, or comparison. Reject compulsory theme/fingerprint rotation and asymmetry for its own sake; repetition can support recognition. |
| Typography | Anthropic and Impeccable `typeset`, supported by Hallmark's role/measure guidance | Explicit roles, few purposeful families, size/weight/spacing hierarchy, real-copy wrapping, language support, font fallback and delivery checks. Reject mandatory pairings, banned system fonts, fixed display ceilings, italic bans, and numerical “taste laws.” |
| Rhythm and grouping | Impeccable `layout` and Hallmark `layout-and-space` | Tight within groups, stronger separation between groups; reuse tokens without making every gap identical. Keep justified optical adjustments. Reject mandatory grid-breaking and nested-card bans detached from content. |
| Palette and depth | Hallmark's role-based palette construction; Impeccable's distinction between restrained task UI and expressive surfaces | Semantic roles and independently checked themes. Hue-related neutrals and OKLCH are options. Reject pure-black/white bans, accent-area quotas, default tinted paper, and automatic dark-mode weight changes. |
| Content and proof | Hallmark's explicit invented-metrics guard; Anthropic's consistent action vocabulary; Impeccable's distinction between authored demonstration and product truth | Preserve factual copy; never invent proof; label illustrative data or choose a different structure. No fake customer logos or performance metrics to satisfy a layout. |
| Scope and authority | Impeccable: missing DESIGN.md does not erase an incumbent identity; polish differs from redesign. Hallmark: preserve implementation boundaries. | Keep existing authority and local scope; authorized redesign replaces only the selected visual layer. Do not force new design documents, token exports, or asset/persistence machinery. |
| Resilience | Impeccable `harden` and Hallmark responsive gates identify long-content, flex/grid, overlay, and mobile failure modes | Test content extremes and fix structural overflow. Reject global `overflow-x: clip` as a compliance patch and no-wrapping clickable-text rules; essential content and focus must remain reachable. |
| Critique | All three use self-critique; Impeccable distinguishes design assessment from mechanical detection | Inspect real renders and the complete task path; report defects separately from aesthetic alternatives. Reject self-scored stamps as proof, mandatory detector/subagent infrastructure, arbitrary screenshot ceilings that waive defects, and indefinite polish. |
| Delegated taste | Local requirement: the frontend owner chooses without requiring immediate visual comparisons | One reasoned provisional direction; explicit approvals/corrections can later calibrate it. No claimed favorite palette or style, silent “approval,” randomized concept deal, compulsory question round, or preset taste sliders. This is local policy, not a demonstrated upstream quality result. |

The shared entrypoint retains production state ownership, composition, responsive, accessibility, motion, dependency, and browser-verification responsibilities. Generic React snippets and arbitrary line-count/prop-depth mandates were replaced by stack-neutral rules. The illustrative modal snippet did not implement its claimed modality; the replacement names native `showModal()` or an established accessible primitive. The accessibility reference also corrects the large-text threshold and distinguishes a comfortable mobile target from a WCAG AA minimum.

Technical checks on the same retrieval date used [W3C text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [W3C target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), and [MDN showModal](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLDialogElement/showModal). Hallmark's APCA preference does not replace the applicable WCAG contrast requirement.

### Local taste-authority clarification

The design language is not limited to these candidates and may mix them with other influences or original work. The engineer's taste determines which aesthetic dimensions need tuning and their desired values; the comparison does not select a fixed style, palette, or parameter preset. Coherence is an outcome check, not a single-lineage rule. This explicit clarification supersedes earlier one-lineage language in the catalog and historical admission evidence. Task usability and engineering constraints remain independent requirements.

### Evidence limits

This comparison establishes source-backed decision differences and rejects concrete conflicts; it does not establish that any candidate produces better-looking pages, that the synthesis matches an individual's visual taste, or that source-identical host deployments behave identically. No visual preference tournament, generated imagery, upstream executable, or paid inference was required for source intake. The repository's frontend evaluation receipt records the checks actually run. Rendered UI quality still requires a real frontend task; personal taste remains provisional until explicit feedback supplies evidence.
