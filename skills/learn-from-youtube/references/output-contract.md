# Teaching artifact contract

## User outcome

Given a video of any kind, produce a coherent learning page containing all useful information recoverable from it within the user's requested scope; default to the whole video, not a highlights selection. The learner should be able to understand its useful ideas and reproduce its demonstrated procedures without returning to the recording, except for explicitly identified gaps or experiences the page cannot faithfully represent. Remove filler, not instructional substance. Extraction logs and coverage counts are supporting evidence, not the deliverable.

Let the source determine the balance of explanation, readable source images, annotated screenshots and ordered visual sequences. A spoken lecture may be transcription-heavy; a silent demonstration may be frame-heavy; many sources require both. Audio events, timing and motion can also carry meaning: preserve their relevant contribution or state what cannot be conveyed. Do not force a fixed screenshot quota, transcript-first treatment or identical page structure onto every video.

Fill small prerequisite or source-quality gaps with verified supplemental explanation, clearly distinguished from recovered source content. For large gaps, identify exactly what remains missing and provide targeted learning references when available; do not bury a replacement course inside the page or imply that links complete the missing lesson. An unresolved central gap prevents claiming a standalone replacement. Do not invent unreadable settings, missing actions or unclear speech.

## Editorial freedom

Write a standalone article for its intended reader, not a source inspection report. Choose its structure from the material: a reproducible tutorial needs an achievable result and a usable sequence; an experimental demo or argument needs context, explanation and appropriately limited conclusions. A selected excerpt still needs the surrounding setup that makes it meaningful.

There is no required sequence of sections, glossary, decision table or exercise quota. Preserve useful introductory context; remove only filler. Headings and figures should communicate the ideas before the reader studies the details.

Keep acquisition details, correction history, hashes and review status in companion records. Provide source links and consequential uncertainty where the reader needs them. Attribute claims naturally (for example, “the creator reports”); distinguish verified additions and inference without mandatory bold category labels on every paragraph.

## Quality gate

Compare the reading experience with a reviewed article of the same kind, not only an artifact checklist. Skim the headings and figures, then read without the video or audit history: does the reader know what this is for, follow the explanation, and reach the intended understanding or result? Use the frontend owner for rendering and image interactions rather than prescribing a visual template here.

Verify coverage against the ledger and claims against evidence. A missing central claim, invented timestamp, source/research confusion or unmarked uncertainty is a critical failure. Mechanical checks do not establish teaching quality or user acceptance.
