# Agent Sync system requirements

Agreed project contract, 2026-09-27. This records expected outcomes, not a claim that they are implemented or an instruction to install a new governance framework.

Agent Sync assembles independently owned governance, authoring, reconciliation, and synchronization capabilities. Together they keep agents useful, consistent, and maintainable without requiring repeated audits of the complete setup. These requirements apply to skills, standing instructions, tool definitions and schemas, and their supported host adapters. Domain implementations retain their own owners.

## Owner map

Every expected behavior has one accountable owner. Supporting owners exchange evidence and artifacts rather than maintain competing policies. These roles should be apparent from the project entry point and implementation boundaries; the project may be reorganized for clarity. A rename is optional, not a prerequisite.

| Owner | Accountable outcome | Current places to inspect, not prescribed final structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governance | Decide what belongs, what evidence permits a change, and whether the system improves user outcomes. | `contracts/ownership.json`, `registry.json`, `tools/capability_intake.py`, `skills/cross-agent-surface-engineering/` |
| Authoring | Improve the owning instruction or tool interface without losing useful behavior. | `skills/instruction-authoring/`; domain/tool owners for executable contracts; Codex native skill-creator for scaffolding/UI only |
| Reconciliation | Resolve differences and dependencies into a scoped, authorized change set. | `tools/reconcile.py`, `contracts/instruction-surfaces.json`, `contracts/surface-matrix.json` |
| Sync | Apply the selected changes, preserve recoverability, and verify the installed and published result. | `tools/fleet.py`, `tools/recovery.py`, `tools/sync_git.py`, host adapters |

These are responsibility boundaries, not requirements for four services, separate repositories, fixed folder names, or duplicated coordination layers. Existing combined modules can remain when their interfaces and responsibilities are clear.

## Claim-based testing and retirement

This governs future skill-value and retirement comparisons under R02, R07, R13 and R14. Use each skill's source-backed stated claim, intended scope and existing ownership-purpose record. Mark missing or inferred claims explicitly; do not invent a claim or a new justification after seeing results.

1. **Exercise the claim.** Choose representative real tasks from the intended work, including material exceptions and cases where the skill should add no intervention. Measure user-relevant outcomes and exercise actual tools/programs when the claim depends on them. Give outcome requirements and necessary constraints without supplying the skill's solution. A convenient synthetic repair, structural pass or test count alone does not establish the skill's broader value.
2. **Check the claimed failure independently.** Map each material claim to an observable outcome and a check outside the evaluated agent's editable answer/tests. Establish that the check rejects the relevant known-bad behavior and accepts an adequate outcome. Inspect the complete result path, important failure/unknown states and leakage or implementation coupling. A checker's independence and its meaningful coverage are separate requirements. Trace every scored assertion to a task requirement or explicit interface/safety constraint; distinguish behavioral invariants from optional keys, layout, algorithm, wording and implementation choices. Exactness requires that source-backed constraint, not similarity to a preferred solution. Select risk-relevant valid alternative solutions and plausible broken controls: the same oracle must accept the former and reject the latter. Explain when an alternative is inapplicable; do not enumerate variants without a material risk. Review semantic controls independently where execution cannot decide them.
3. **Declare comparability before running.** Record the claims/cases, required quality and hard constraints, practical quality/cost tolerances, evidence adequacy, trial/order plan, budget and stopping rule before inference. Choose tolerances for the task; do not invent universal numbers or adjust them to rescue a skill. Failure to detect a difference, a ceiling task or an underpowered sample is not evidence of comparability. Freeze the oracle, controls and requirement mapping before trials, outside actor inputs. Preserve raw outcomes. Any postcapture unblinded oracle correction must retain the original scores and separately identify the defect, affected outcomes, countercontrols and interpretation limits; never tune an oracle to admit a preferred arm.
4. **Hold the comparison equivalent.** Run with and without the target skill under matched task inputs, model/settings, native runtime/tools, context, resource limits and independent checks. Preserve common safety/protocol requirements. Remove the target body and its leaked hints from the without-skill condition. Test natural discovery or orchestration normally when that is the claim; explicit injection or a simplified leaf cannot stand in for those mechanisms. Record actual exposure, settings and deviations rather than relying on requested labels.
5. **Account for the whole job.** Report quality alongside overall root, worker, judge, support, retry and failed-attempt tokens/calls, plus time and human correction effort where relevant. Separate one-time setup/correction from recurring execution, but retain both costs. State cached-token semantics and attribution. Missing usage or billing remains unknown, not zero; incomplete required accounting cannot establish cost comparability or a saving.
6. **Make the evidence-based decision.** When valid, sufficiently informative comparisons cover the skill's material claims and show comparable outcomes without it under the declared quality/cost criteria, retire the skill through the existing ownership, dependency and recovery process. Passing with the skill is not a reason to retain it. A claimed cost benefit must itself be measured; do not invent one after a quality tie. Invalid, mismatched, missing or inconclusive evidence remains unassessed, not proof of no added value or authority to remove a skill. A result covering one narrow behavior cannot retire an entire multi-claim skill; untested claims must be documented existing claims, not post-hoc excuses for retention.

Record the scope, quality/cost result, uncertainty and retirement rationale in existing evaluation/ownership records. Existing authorization and safe application boundaries still apply. Historical scores keep their original scope: this contract does not upgrade a narrow diagnostic or authorize a new trial, publication, instruction change or actual retirement by itself. Reuse the existing evaluator and retirement consumers; do not create another testing framework for this contract.

## Desired result and limits

The system detects a concrete problem, routes it to the owner, completes and checks the intended outcome using the simplest adequate mechanism by total system complexity and cost, reconciles only authorized dependencies, and verifies deployment. Patch size does not determine simplicity. Existing/native solutions and no change remain valid choices.

Undesired outcomes include recurring complete-agent audits, a central policy monolith, duplicated owners, speculative capability claims, mechanical compression that loses knowledge, trigger overreach, scope creep, unrelated-work coupling, silent regressions, and governance whose overhead exceeds its benefit.

Contraindications include insufficient evidence for a claimed improvement, ambiguous ownership, inseparable conflicting edits, unsupported host mappings, incompatible schema callers, and actions beyond authorization. These conditions justify a specific pending decision or bounded investigation, not blanket blocking of independent approved work.

## Model autonomy and the Hermes replacement decision, 2026-10-14

Status: accepted design direction; replacement readiness remains conditional. User authority: the parallel action request in Codex chat `01a1370f-dd39-7640-8c00-2e6d621d6aae`, including its clarification that Codex and Codex Dots remain in use. OMP is the prospective Hermes replacement. This decision extends R02, R05, R09 and R14; it does not change provider, spending, permissions or runtime model defaults.

Retain capabilities that supply facts, perform useful operations or enforce deterministic guarantees. Keep scope, task state, authorization, budgets, cancellation, stale-edit rejection, truthful results and recovery in their executable owners. Within those boundaries, let the model choose its method. Tool availability is not an obligation to use that tool. Keep core preferences available; load substantial task-specific instructions and specialist documentation when needed. Measure actually loaded context and induced actions, including discovery, reviews, retries and compaction, rather than source length or tool count.

Instructions are useful when they carry required human preferences, missing domain knowledge or necessary protocols. General coaching and duplicated methods have no automatic claim on the prompt. A runtime defect belongs in the runtime or tool; a shorter prompt cannot repair broken grants, dispatch, cancellation or persistence. The existing authoring owner's instruction-modality and necessity rules already cover this distinction. The shared standing change is limited to extending optional guidance selection to tools; no new skill or recurring procedure is needed.

### Why this mechanism

The strongest practical alternative is a smaller Pi runtime with selected extensions. Pi's code mode supplies broad tool composition and persistent explicit JSON state; this is a credible alternative, not an inferior baseline. OMP already supplies useful LSP, protected edits, persistent Python/Bun execution, deferred specialists and extension hooks. Its supported system-template customization can separate those capabilities from mandatory method prescriptions without first maintaining a private fork. Prefer that reversible seam while its contracts remain adequate. Reconsider Pi if maintaining OMP's independent injected owners becomes more costly than supplying the missing capabilities there.

OMP's direct and mounted-tool availability gates can activate standing routing rules before specialist schemas are loaded. Schema deferral therefore does not by itself defer induced actions. The native OMP workstream owns template/settings corrections and assembly checks. Agent Sync owns shared preferences, portable skill admission, host adapters, loading records and recovery. Integrate a native candidate only after its checked loading, precedence, tool/runtime and rollback contracts are known. Do not copy the entire Hermes catalogue into OMP to claim retention.

Other alternatives are rejected for specific reasons: a private OMP fork adds upgrade responsibility before the supported seam is exhausted; disabling specialists loses useful facts and operations without addressing standing obligations; more manager or specialist bots duplicate Codex Dots and do not improve runtime orchestration. Blanket model-specific exceptions are unsupported. Frontier-model quality harm, prompt interference, speed and total-cost savings remain hypotheses. The Composio Pi/OMP result uses another model and has provider/effort confounds. It motivates inspection but establishes no Sol verdict. Image rerolls, toy all-pass suites and a broad model sweep do not settle this decision.
