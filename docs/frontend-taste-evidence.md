# Frontend skills, Astra and visual choice

## Corrected research conclusion

The earlier source-level audit did not investigate current practitioner reports sufficiently. It could identify prescriptive wording in the installed skill, but could not establish that the skill helped Astra or dismiss reports of harm. The selected change is user-led visual choice and less compulsory procedure, not a claimed measured improvement in model design quality.

No local model-quality experiment was run for this change. No third-party style pack was installed. Visual selection gives the user authority over taste without assuming that more prompt text is better.

The Chase article and both Reddit threads were fetched, not only accepted from search snippets. The MindStudio limitation appeared in retrieved search excerpts. Results about an Astra-branded theme/framework/security company were excluded. Private communities, unindexed posts and a corpus-wide prevalence estimate were not covered. These sources justify a narrower operational decision, not a claim that the search found all reports.

## Durable decisions

- Existing production accessibility/state/verification guidance remains; stylistic recipes are conditional.
- Unresolved taste gets quick comparable visual options, with explore-more links and an option to delegate.
- The user can keep, combine, reject or refine qualities instead of selecting an entire branded skill.
- No compulsory visual-thesis schema, fixed refinement order or full viewport matrix for every tiny edit.
- A model-quality claim still needs suitable comparative evidence. User-selected workflow preferences do not need to masquerade as research findings.
